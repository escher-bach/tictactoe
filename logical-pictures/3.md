## 0. The thesis

What learners converge to is a *structure*: objects, the forms that say how objects may combine, and the facts that hold of them. It is identified up to isomorphism. A vector representation is a numerical representation of that structure. It is identified only up to a continuous group, and it records less.

---

## 1. Two Platonisms

**The vector one.** A context is a collection of vectors; computation is linear maps and pointwise functions; with tied weights the output is the similarity of the last vector to a code book. The Platonic representation hypothesis says different learners converge on one kernel. Balestriero and LeCun make it a theorem for JEPAs: the optimum is the top-k spectral embedding of the co-occurrence operator K_π, unique up to rotation; for next-token prediction, up to GL(k). The distributional hypothesis is underneath.

**The logical one.** Three claims.

- **LP1 (structure).** The Platonic representation of a world is a relational structure 𝕎, identified up to isomorphism.
- **LP2 (picture).** The representation of a context is a finite structure, a *picture*, with a map into 𝕎. Computing with representations is operating on structures: extend, identify, restrict, count interpretations. The continuation is not computed *from* the picture. It is *in* it: a picture of a prefix is partial, and the probability of a continuation is the share of the picture's completions that contain it (TLP 5.15).
- **LP3 (shadow).** Any vector representation learners agree on is an isomorphism-invariant function of 𝕎 that loses information. The vector gauge (O(k), GL(k)) is an artefact of the loss.

## 2. What the picture theory demands, and what is dogma

### 2.1 The clauses

| | Clause | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Names and objects.** Signs stand for objects, and the same object can be named in many places. An object is simple: nothing is true of it but which combinations it *can* enter (its form) and which it *does* enter | TLP 3.202–3.203, 2.0141 |
| **P2** | **States of affairs.** An elementary fact is a combination of objects. A sign-fact depicts by its own arrangement: *that* "a" stands in a relation to "b" says that aRb | TLP 2.01, 3.1432 |
| **P3** | **Recombination.** Given the objects, all possible states of affairs are given. Whatever the forms permit is possible | TLP 2.0124; Armstrong 1989 |
| **P4** | **Independence.** From one elementary fact nothing follows about another | TLP 2.061–2.062, 5.134 |
| **P5** | **Showing.** The form of a picture is shown, not said. A picture has exactly as many distinguishable parts as what it depicts | TLP 2.172, 4.04 |
| **P6** | **Probability.** The probability r gives s is the share of r's truth-grounds that are truth-grounds of s. Probability is needed only where the picture is incomplete but its form is known | TLP 5.15, 5.156 |
| **P7** | **Series.** A series is generated by applying an operation to its own result; this is the concept "and so on" | TLP 5.2521–5.2523 |

P7 is the Tractatus's account of autoregression. It is also the exact target of the rule-following remarks (§6).

### 2.2 The recantation, taken as the dogma audit

Wittgenstein's own diagnosis of the Tractatus is that it "predicated of the thing what lay in the mode of representing it" (PI §104): simple names and independent elementary propositions were a *requirement*, not a finding (Baker and Hacker, essay XIII, in the Wittgenstein folder). His conclusion was that the ideal "loses none of its dignity" once it is recognised as a form of representation. That is the only standing the picture form has here. The theory fixes a *kind* of representation and asks, of a given corpus, whether an analysis of that kind exists and is unique. The answer can be no.

| Tractatus | The objection | Status here |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis uncovers hidden simples, a priori | A requirement projected onto language (PI §§89–108). By 1929 analysis is already "in a certain sense a posteriori" (RLF) | Objects are learned from the corpus. Existence and uniqueness of an analysis are open per corpus |
| Elementary propositions are independent | Colour exclusion: "a is red" excludes "a is blue". Propositions come in systems laid against reality like a ruler (RLF; *Satzsysteme*) | Values are grouped into **determinables**: families of mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive values. Independence is asked between determinables, never between values of one |
| Absolute simples | Simple is relative to a language-game (PI §§47–48). What "must exist" is a sample that belongs to the language (PI §50) | An object is a class of occurrences under a learned criterion. "Simple" means: not split by any difference the corpus shows. Standards are samples taken from the corpus (§5.6) |
| The meaning of a name is its bearer | Meaning is use (PI §43) | An object is nothing beyond its criterion of identity and its record of what recurs with it |
| Sense is determinate | Vagueness is not hidden exactness | Exceptions are measured ranges of facts the text does not show (TLP 5.156). No hidden exact rule is assumed |
| "And so on" is given with the operation | PI §§185–242; Kripke | §6 |
| Every proposition is a picture | Language has many uses | Scope is limited to the predictive structure of a corpus. Following Clancey: pictures are built per context and never stored as retrievable descriptions |

## 3. Two lemmas

### 3.1 Lemma A: the propositional part is empty

**Lemma A [P].** Let P be any distribution on strings of length n whose probabilities are multiples of 2⁻ᴷ. There are K elementary propositions, independent, each giving every other probability ½ (TLP 5.152), and for each string u a truth-function S_u of them ("the text begins with u"), such that the Tractarian probability S_u gives S_ua is P(ua)/P(u).

*Proof.* Let ω range over {0,1}ᴷ, read as an integer, with p_i = "bit i of ω is 1". List the strings in lexicographic order and give w the next P(w)·2ᴷ integers. Let S_u hold at ω when ω falls in the block of some w beginning with u. That block is an interval of P(u)·2ᴷ points, every set of points is a truth-function of the p_i, and the ratio of truth-grounds is P(ua)/P(u). ∎

Non-dyadic P is approximated to within 2⁻ᴷ. This is arithmetic coding read backwards: any model is a decoder of fair coin flips.

**Consequence.** P4, P6 and truth-functionality together forbid nothing. A transformer language model *is* a Tractarian logical space in this sense. So a picture theory says something only through P1–P3 and P5: the elementary facts must be facts about *objects*, and the text must *picture* them. The bits of an arithmetic code are not about anything.

### 3.2 Lemma B: induction is re-identification

**Lemma B [P].** Let the elementary facts be independent, e depend only on facts in a set E, and h only on facts in H. If E and H are disjoint, e gives h its prior probability.

*Proof.* Product measure. ∎

So if a prefix changes the probability of a continuation, every analysis into independent facts must give the two a common elementary fact. In a fully analysed language there are no correlations between distinct facts and "no causal nexus" (TLP 5.136). **The past informs the future only because the future mentions the same objects.**

**[Reading]** This is what Armstrong's theory of laws contributes. A regularity is not many independent instances that happen to agree. It is one state of affairs between universals, N(F, G), present in each instance. The next instance is not a new fact that might differ; it is the same object again. Counting, weight sharing and in-context learning are all this one thing.

### 3.3 The collapse list as one table

By Lemma B, a predictive model is characterised by its **criterion of identity**: when do two occurrences count as showing the same object?

| Family | "The same object again" means | Scope | Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| n-gram, class n-gram | the same literal (or class-of-literal) window of fixed length | corpus-wide only | objects inside a text; a learned criterion; composition |
| PPM, LZ, caches, infini-gram | the longest literal match | both, one fixed criterion | abstraction: the key is always the raw sign |
| HMM, automata, causal states | the same state: a class of whole prefixes under a right congruence | corpus-wide only | particulars. Nothing in the text is an object, so a string seen once cannot be named again |
| PCFG, grammar induction, phrase trees | the same nonterminal; each span has one parent | corpus-wide, tree-shaped | re-entrancy: an object cannot occur in two states of affairs |
| Brown clustering, class bigram | the same class of the previous sign | corpus-wide | everything after one abstraction |
| EM | not a criterion: a way to fit measures inside a fixed form | | the form |
| ILP, program synthesis | not a criterion: a search over definitions | | a rule that produces candidates from the data |
| Transformer | learned, several per position, composed in layers, graded | both | (the competitor) |

**[Def] Collapse.** A theory has collapsed when its criteria of identity are fixed in advance, or all come from one row above. Not collapsing needs four things together: criteria that are learned; objects of both scopes; criteria defined over objects already constructed; several objects named at one position.

## 4. Logical space with objects

### 4.1 Form is exchangeability

P1 says an object has no nature beyond its form. Two objects of the same form differ only in being two (TLP 2.0233). Formally: the law of the world is invariant under any permutation of objects that preserves form. Names are arbitrary. This is the one symmetry the theory assumes, and it is the same condition the earlier work imposed as "the rule commutes with relabelling the alphabet".

### 4.2 The Tractarian representation theorem

**Theorem (Aldous 1981, Hoover 1979) [K].** Let (X_ij) be a random array whose law is invariant under permuting the index set (jointly exchangeable). Then there are independent uniform random variables α, (ξ_i), (ζ_ij) and a fixed function f with

> X_ij = f(α, ξ_i, ξ_j, ζ_ij).

De Finetti's theorem is the one-index case, X_i = f(α, ξ_i). Higher arities and several sorts work the same way (Kallenberg).

**[Reading]** This is the picture theory as a theorem. If names are arbitrary, the observed relations are a *deterministic picture* (f) of *independent elementary facts*, and those facts attach to exactly three kinds of thing:

| Aldous–Hoover | Armstrong | In a learner |
|---|---|---|
| α: one fact shared by everything | universals and the laws between them | weights: what training fixed |
| ξ_i: one fact per object | particulars | in-context variables: what this text fixed |
| ζ_ij: one fact per configuration | states of affairs | the residue: what nothing fixes |

Lemma A gave universality without objects, and it was empty. This theorem gives it *with* objects, under one hypothesis that can fail. Two cautions. The theorem treats α as one fact; analysing it into many independent universals, each involved in few states of affairs, is what a learned form has to do. And the positions of a text are ordered, not exchangeable, so the theorem applies to the hidden sorts and not to positions (open problem 7).

Carnap's inductive logic is the one-index case with a choice of prior: m\* is uniform on structure descriptions (isomorphism classes), and the λ-continuum admits that the choice is not forced by logic. That admission is kept here (§5.5).

### 4.3 Definitions

- **[Def] Form.** A finite set of *sorts* including Pos. For each sort, *determinables* A : s → D_A with D_A a finite set of values, and *links* g : s → s′ to other sorts. A state of affairs with several constituents is itself an object, with one link per constituent (Armstrong: states of affairs are particulars).
- **[Def] World.** A finite set of objects for each sort, a linear order on Pos, and a value for every determinable and link. Objects other than positions have no names: worlds isomorphic over Pos are the same world.
- **[Def] Elementary fact.** A(o) = v, or g(o) = o′.
- **[Def] Logical space** of a form: all its worlds. P3 is the statement that *all* of them are possible. P4 and P6 are the statement that, in a fully analysed form, values of distinct (object, determinable) pairs are independent and uniform.

A determinable with its value set is a Satzsystem. A world is a point in a product of scales. That is the middle-period logical space: a coordinate space with privileged axes and no rotations.

### 4.4 Probability is counting interpretations

A picture A is true in a world 𝕎 in as many ways as there are structure-preserving maps A → 𝕎. So TLP 5.15 reads:

> P(continuation | prefix) = #{maps of the extended picture into 𝕎} / #{maps of the prefix picture into 𝕎},

or the corresponding ratio of measures over worlds. Two degenerate cases locate the classical methods:

- **Path pictures in the raw corpus.** If the picture of a context is a chain of n − 1 positions with their signs, and 𝕎 is the corpus itself, the ratio is count(context · a) / count(context): the n-gram estimate. With 𝕎 a quotient of the corpus by classes it is a class n-gram. **[P]** by inspection.
- **Structure without names.** If the picture keeps only the order and which positions carry the *same* sign, "abab" matches "xyxy" anywhere in the corpus and the ratio predicts "a". This is Carnap's purely structural description (*Aufbau* §§11–16) doing in-context prediction by analogy.

Everything between these two is the theory's subject: pictures that are not chains, in worlds that are not the raw corpus.

## 5. The picture of a text

### 5.1 The text-fact

**[Def]** A text is the fact T = (Pos, <, ≈): positions, their order, and the relation "carries the same sign". By TLP 3.1432 a text depicts through the arrangement of its signs, and arrangement plus sameness is all a text has. The earlier work proved the matching statement: equality is the only relation between symbols invariant under relabelling, so it is the only one that supplies nothing.

### 5.2 Sorts are abstraction principles

**[Def] Criterion of identity.** A sort s other than Pos is given by a link name_s : Pos → s and an equivalence φ_s on positions with

> name_s(t) = name_s(u) ⟺ φ_s(t, u),

where φ_s is decidable from the picture strictly before the later of t and u. Objects of sort s are the φ_s-classes.

This is Frege's "the direction of a = the direction of b iff a ∥ b", Carnap's quasi-analysis (objects as classes under a relation among experiences), and the model theorist's imaginary sort (the quotient of a structure by a definable equivalence). "No entity without identity" is taken literally: to learn an object is to learn when it is the same one.

**[Def] Scope.** A criterion is *universal* if it identifies positions across texts, *particular* if only within one text. Universals carry what the corpus fixed. Particulars are new in every text.

Training analyses the corpus-fact. Inference pictures a new text in the form already found. In-context learning is the particulars.

### 5.3 Determinables are partitions too

A determinable A on sort s, pulled back along name_s, partitions the positions by value. Its values are mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive over positions that name an s-object: an **exact transversal** in the sense of the Carnap project next door, where a place is a family of qualities with exactly one member in every experience. So sorts and determinables are the same kind of thing, a partition of occurrences, and the fact "A is a determinable of sort s" is a refinement:

> φ_s ≤ ker A (positions naming the same s-object show the same A-value).

**[Def]** The apparatus therefore lives in the lattice of partitions of Pos: join is conjunction of criteria, meet is the closure of chains of identifications, and facts are refinements. This is the logic of distinctions, dual to the Boolean logic of propositions (Ellerman's partition logic). A partition has no labels, so there is no code gauge at all. The cube codes {±1}ᵏ of the earlier attempt are the special case of k two-valued determinables.

### 5.5 Reading the sign off the picture

**[Def] Code.** A form designates k sign paths (name_{s_i}, A_i). The *code* of position t is (A_1(name_{s_1}(t)), …, A_k(name_{s_k}(t))). The world pictures the text when two positions carry the same sign exactly when they have the same code. A fact A_i(name_{s_i}(t)) is **new** at t if no earlier position names that object, and **repeated** otherwise. The **residue** is the list of new facts.

**Proposition 5.1 (size is surprisal) [P].** In a fully analysed form with exact criteria and signs in bijection with codes,

> P(T) = ∏ over new facts of 1/|D_A|, so −log P(T) = Σ over new facts of log |D_A|.

*Proof.* Generate the text left to right. Repeated coordinates are determined. Each new coordinate is a fresh uniform value by P4 and P6. ∎

The surprisal of a text is the size of its picture's residue. This is Carnap and Bar-Hillel's semantic information: the amount of logical space the text excludes. Next-sign probability is TLP 5.15: uniform over the signs consistent with the repeated coordinates.

**Exceptions.** Real criteria are not exact. Where an occurrence contradicts the object its criterion assigns, the picture was wrong: it is a different object (§7, move M2). The frequency of that is the measure of a range of facts the text does not show (TLP 5.156). **Numbers enter the theory in two roles only: counts, and measures of ranges. They never locate a meaning.** Carnap's λ sits here: one non-logical number per determinable, unless the tower removes it (open problem 5).

**Proposition 5.2 (the tied read-out) [P].** Let signs have codes c(w) ∈ {±1}ᵏ and let the picture give a partial description h ∈ {−1, 0, +1}ᵏ of the next sign (0 = not determined). Suppose coordinate i of the description is wrong independently with probability ε_i < ½. Then

> log P(w | h) = const + Σ_i β_i h_i c_i(w), with β_i = ½ log((1 − ε_i)/ε_i).

As ε → 0 the maximisers are exactly the signs that satisfy every determined coordinate: the description's range.

*Proof.* The likelihood of h_i given c_i(w) is 1 − ε_i if they agree and ε_i if not; take logs and collect terms. ∎

So the weight-tied inner product is the degree to which each name's object satisfies a partial description, and a coordinate's magnitude is the log-odds of its exception range. This is the logical reading of "the last vector is compared with the code book": the code book is a coordinate system on the vocabulary, the same on the way in and on the way out.

## 6. "And so on"

### 6.1 Autoregression as a Kripke frame

Take the pictures of prefixes as stages, ordered by one-point extension. This is a Kripke frame of the kind used for intuitionistic logic: a stage is a state of information, facts once shown stay shown, and a stage *forces* a fact when every admissible extension shows it. A text is a path through the frame. It is a choice sequence in the sense Kripke gave Brouwer's notion: generated in time, partly bound by law and partly free.

**[Def]** The lawlike part of a text is its repeated facts. The lawless part is its residue. A sequence is exactly as lawless as its residue, and by Proposition 5.1 that quantity is its surprisal. Criteria of identity are defined stage by stage on strictly earlier stages, so the definition is grounded in the way Kripke's fixed-point construction of truth is grounded: nothing refers forward.

### 6.2 The paradox, for a learner

TLP 5.2523 says the concept of successive application is the concept "and so on". PI §185 and Kripke's reconstruction deny that anything in a finite past fixes what "the same" operation yields next: every finite prefix fits a rule that agrees so far and deviates later ("quus"). Kripke notes that this is Goodman's new riddle applied to meaning. For a learner it is the plain fact that data underdetermine generalisation. Kripke's own verdict on two tempting answers transfers directly: dispositions do not help (a learner's dispositions are finite and include its errors), and simplicity does not help (simplicity is relative to a language, and the language is what is in question).

### 6.3 What the picture theory says

It does not produce the "superlative fact" the sceptic asks for. It moves the question to where something can be checked.

1. **A rule is a criterion of identity.** By Lemma B the continuation is determined exactly when the next case is *the same object* as the earlier ones. The sceptic's "what makes it the same?" is answered by the criterion φ_s, and φ_s is a norm of the representation, adopted and not discovered. "This is simply what I do" (PI §217) is the correct description of a criterion.
2. **It is not arbitrary, because it must be shown.** A criterion is admitted only on a record of repeated facts in the corpus (§7). That is Goodman's entrenchment in constructive form: the projectible predicates are the ones built by abstraction from shown identities.
3. **Quus is not refuted. It is unsayable.** A quus-like continuation needs a distinction among objects (those below 57 and the rest) that no shown fact supports. Under exchangeability the language has no determinable separating them until the corpus shows one. Hypotheses are recombinations of objects the language has, not arbitrary functions.
4. **Correctness is agreement.** Kripke's sceptical solution rests meaning on agreement within a community. For a learner the corpus is the community's practice. Across learners, agreement is the Platonic representation hypothesis itself, and it is Carnap's intersubjectivity (*Aufbau* §§146–148): the objective is what is invariant across subjects' constructional systems.

**[Reading]** I did not find a text of Armstrong's in the folders. Item 1 is my reconstruction of how his combinatorialism and his theory of laws bear on rule-following; treat it as that. A vector learner is in the same position as this one: its inductive bias is its form of life. The difference is that here the bias is a list of criteria that can be read.

## 7. What tame logic guarantees

*(§7.4 of the original; the rest of §7, the learning rule, is left out.)*

The lesson of 21st-century model theory for this project is that learnability is a tameness property of a theory, and that under tameness the logician's objects exist and are small.

- **NIP is learnability [K].** A definable family has finite VC dimension in every model iff the theory lacks the independence property (Laskowski 1992). A stable theory is one whose definable families have finite Littlestone dimension, which is online learnability (Chase and Freitag 2019).
- **Few types [K].** In an NIP family the number of types over n samples is polynomial in n (Sauer–Shelah). In a wild family it is 2ⁿ. So types over samples (§5.6) cannot shatter in the tame case.
- **Few witnesses [K].** Finite-VC classes have sample compression schemes of bounded size (Moran and Yehudayoff 2016); in NIP theories types over finite sets are uniformly definable (Chernikov and Simon 2015); in stable theories every type is definable. A bounded set of samples fixes an object's whole relational profile.
- **Counts are legitimate evidence [K].** In NIP theories the well-behaved measures on logical space are exactly those approximated by averages over finitely many points (Hrushovski, Pillay and Simon 2013). This is the modern form of Carnap's programme: a measure on logical space, learned by counting, with a theorem saying when counting converges uniformly.
- **A margin [K].** In a stable relation there are regular partitions with polynomially many classes, no irregular pairs, and every density within ε of 0 or 1 (Malliaris and Shelah 2014). For bounded VC dimension the same holds for all but an ε-fraction of pairs (Alon, Fischer and Newman 2007; Lovász and Szegedy 2010; Fox, Pach and Suk 2019). Exact indiscernibility has no margin and shatters under noise; these are the versions with ε.

**What these do not give.** They are existence theorems. They do not say that M1–M3 with the candidates of §7.2 find the witnesses in near-linear time. Structure that shows only when r things are held together costs on the order of n^r for any procedure that tests candidates one at a time, which is the boundary the earlier attempt found it shares with gradient descent. The logician has no exemption.

## 8. Vectors as shadows

### 8.1 The read-out

Proposition 5.2: the tied inner product is satisfaction of a partial description under independent exceptions. **[P]**

### 8.2 The gauge gap

**Proposition 8.1 [P].** Let Z = {±1}ⁿ with the uniform law, and let a positive pair agree on each coordinate independently with probability (1 + λ)/2, 0 < λ < 1. Then:

1. K_π = K₁ ⊗ … ⊗ K_n, and its eigenfunctions are the characters χ_S(z) = ∏_{i∈S} z_i with eigenvalues λ^{|S|}.
2. The top non-constant eigenspace is spanned by the n coordinate functions χ₁, …, χ_n, with gap λ − λ². So the n-dimensional spectral (JEPA) representation is h(z) = Q·(z₁, …, z_n) for an *arbitrary* Q ∈ O(n), and nothing in the objective prefers one Q.
3. A function in that span takes exactly two values iff it is ±c·χ_i for some i.

*Proof.* (1) Each coordinate's operator is ½[[1+λ, 1−λ],[1−λ, 1+λ]], with eigenvalues 1 and λ; eigenpairs of a tensor product are products. (2) follows from λ > λ² > …. (3) If u·z ∈ {a, b} for all z, then (u·z − m)² is constant for m = (a+b)/2. Expanding, Σ_{i≠j} u_i u_j z_i z_j − 2m Σ_i u_i z_i is constant on the cube. Distinct characters are linearly independent, so u_i u_j = 0 for i ≠ j and m·u_i = 0: u has one non-zero entry and m = 0. ∎

**Meaning.** In this world the logical representation is n propositions, identified up to permuting and negating them: a finite group. The vector Platonic representation is identified up to O(n), a continuous group of dimension n(n−1)/2, and a generic representative mixes every proposition into every coordinate. The logical content is recoverable from the vector one, but only by a condition that is itself logical: *two-valuedness*. The eigengap, which makes the spectral theory well-posed here, does nothing to fix the gauge.

**In general [K].** A connected graph has a unique factorisation into prime factors under the Cartesian product (Sabidussi 1960, Vizing 1963). Read for the graph "differs in one determinable", this says the determinables of a product world are determined by the world, repeated isomorphic factors included. Repeated factors are exactly where eigenvalues are degenerate.

### 8.3 Kernels see walks

**[K]** A structure is determined up to isomorphism by its counts of homomorphisms from all finite structures (Lovász 1967). Restricting the class of test structures gives a hierarchy: counts from cycles determine exactly the spectrum; counts from trees determine exactly what the two-variable counting logic C² and colour refinement can express; counts from structures of treewidth k determine exactly C^{k+1} (Dvořák 2010; Dell, Grohe and Rattan 2018; Grohe 2020). Message-passing networks sit at the tree level.

**[Reading]** This is LP3 in its sharpest known form. Probability in §4.4 is a ratio of homomorphism counts. Counting maps from tree-shaped pictures is multilinear in vectors indexed by the objects of 𝕎, which is why linear maps and pointwise products appear at all: **a position's vector is its table of interpretation counts, indexed by what the position might denote.** Objects of the same form have equal counts, so the dimension needed is the number of forms. A spectral kernel keeps only the walk-shaped part. Two worlds can agree on every tree count and still differ on a first-order sentence (a hexagon and two triangles: only one contains a triangle). What separates them is a picture with a cycle, that is, an object occurring in two places. That is the same re-entrancy whose absence defines the PCFG row of §3.3.

### 8.4 Dimension is a logical invariant

**[K]** In Leitgeb's reconstruction of the *Aufbau*, a quality space has dimension k iff it is (k+1)-Helly and not k-Helly: joint compatibility of a family follows from compatibility of every k+1 members and not from every k. Dimension is the arity of the exclusion grammar of the logical space. A vector-space dimension is a numerical representation of it. The Carnap project next door measured this number on camera data.

### 8.5 Inference is evaluation of formulas

**[K]** Masked unique-hard-attention transformers recognise exactly the star-free languages, which is linear temporal logic over positions, which is first-order logic with order (Yang, Chiang and Angluin 2024; Kamp's theorem). Hard attention is the "since" operator. Averaging and soft attention add counting: temporal counting logics below, first-order logic with majority and TC⁰ above (Barceló et al. 2024; Yang and Chiang 2024; Merrill and Sabharwal 2023). Message passing on graphs is graded modal logic (Barceló et al. 2020).

**[Reading]** So at inference a transformer evaluates a formula of a temporal counting modal logic on the Kripke structure whose worlds are the positions, and each residual coordinate holds a truth value or a count of a definable property. Training selects the formula. The logician's theory replaces the selection procedure, not the object selected.

### 8.6 Where real numbers are needed

**[K + Reading]** The regularity results of §7.4 give a dichotomy. On a stable relation a finite picture with 0/1 relations between classes is correct up to ε, with polynomially many classes. Instability means long half-graphs, that is, a linear order: thresholds and magnitudes. Orders are scales, and the middle-period logical space has scales as a primitive (the ruler). What remains after scales is relation that looks random at every resolution. That has unbounded VC dimension and is not learnable from polynomially many samples by anyone, vector learners included.

### 8.7 The thesis and its truth-condition

**[Thesis]** *After analysis, every relation of the corpus-world between sorts has bounded VC dimension once finitely many linear orders are adjoined.* If so, a logical picture is correct to within ε at polynomial size, vector kernels over it are block-constant up to ε, and the logical representation loses nothing and removes the gauge. If not, some part of the world is irreducibly a table of numbers.

**Predictions that differ from the vector hypothesis** (stated, not tested):

1. **A privileged, mostly two-valued basis.** In the right basis, learned features take few values. Rotation freedom is an artefact (Proposition 8.1), and a method that looks for few-valued directions should recover the same features across models.
2. **Convergence at the level of partitions.** Models should agree on *which occurrences are the same object*, sort by sort. That is stronger than kernel alignment and has a finite gauge.
3. **Continuous features are scales.** Where a feature is truly graded it should be one-dimensional and ordered or cyclic: a ruler. Reports of circular features for days and months are the kind of case this predicts; I cite that from memory.
4. **Dimension tracks a Helly number**, not a variance budget.

## 10. Where the logician is probably wrong

*(Items 1 and 4 of the original, on finding the witnesses and on the uniqueness of the tower, concern the learner and are left out. Original numbering kept.)*

2. **The cancellation gap.** Counting truth-grounds is positive arithmetic. Vectors use signed arithmetic, and signed representations can be exponentially smaller than positive ones: rank against non-negative rank, weighted automata with negative weights against hidden Markov models **[K]**. Negation is a logical operation and Lemma A shows truth-functions can express anything, so the gap is not one of principle. It is one of cost, and it is unpaid. If pictures with negation cannot match signed sums at equal size, the vector side is right on those worlds and LP3 is false there.
3. **Is language tame?** The thesis of §8.7 may fail empirically. It is a definite question about a corpus, which is in its favour, but the answer is not known.
5. **Occupancy.** In vision every place has a colour in every experience, so exact transversals work. In language most determinables are unoccupied at most positions. A graded notion of occupancy is needed and is not constructed here. This was already the stated caveat in the Carnap project.
6. **The exception measure.** One number per determinable (Carnap's λ) is the only non-logical quantity. The tower might remove it, by treating the pattern of exceptions as the next level's corpus. Not shown.
7. **The representation theorem for ordered texts.** Aldous–Hoover needs exchangeable indices. A text's positions are rigid. The right theorem is for structures with one rigid order and exchangeable hidden sorts.
8. **Graded structure as thresholds.** A smooth density such as W(x, y) = x·y is a conjunction of two independent threshold facts, one per object. Whether every low-rank kernel that occurs has such a reading at comparable size is item 2 again.
